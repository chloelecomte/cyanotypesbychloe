5Print the negative▾

Printing tips: print on inkjet transparency film, on the rough (coated) side. Use your printer's best quality, colour mode (not greyscale), and turn colour management off ("Printer manages colours" or "No colour adjustment"). Always print negatives with the same settings you used for your calibration tablet. Let the ink dry before contact printing.

Calibrate for your paper, chemistry and light

The built-in "Generic cyanotype" profile is only an estimate. Cyanotype results vary a lot with paper, sensitizer, UV source, printer and film. A calibration measures your process once, so the curve and the preview match your real prints.

1 · Print the step tablet negative

This is a strip of 21 grey patches, already inverted and mirrored. Print it on transparency film with exactly the settings you'll use for negatives.

2 · Find your exposure time

  1. Expose strips of the tablet for different times (e.g. 5, 10, 15, 20 min in sun or under UV), develop and dry them fully — cyanotype darkens as it dries.
  2. Pick the shortest time at which the last few patches (19–21, clearest film) have all merged into the same deepest blue, while patches 1–2 stay paper white. This "standard printing time" idea is how Christina Z. Anderson approaches it in her cyanotype and digital-negative books.
  3. From now on always use that time, paper and chemistry.

3 · Read your print

Scan or photograph the dry print of the tablet (even light, no colour correction). Patch 1 is the lightest, patch 21 the darkest.

Enter 21 readings from patch 1 (lightest) to 21 (darkest), separated by commas or spaces. Use Lab "L" values (0–100) or grey values (0–255) from an eyedropper.

How digital negatives for cyanotype work

The short version

  1. Edit your photo as a black & white positive that looks the way you want the print to look.
  2. The app inverts it into a negative: light areas of the photo become dark ink that blocks UV, so the paper stays white there.
  3. A curve corrects the negative. Cyanotype only "sees" part of the tonal range — without correction prints come out contrasty, with lost highlights and blocked shadows.
  4. The negative is mirrored so the ink side can touch the paper (sharper prints), then printed on transparency film.

About the preview

The Cyanotype view simulates the print by running your negative through a model of the process. With the Generic profile it's an educated guess; after you calibrate (header button), it uses your measured tones and even the actual blues and paper colour of your scan.

Reading the strip at the bottom

"Ideal print" is a perfectly even 21-step scale in cyanotype colours. "Predicted print" is what your current negative curve would give you. The closer they match, and the more distinct tones you get, the better. Try clicking Linear and then Auto-fit to see the difference.

Good starting photos

Images with clear shapes, good midtone separation and not too many large deep-shadow areas tend to print well. Cyanotype is a fairly short-scale process with a beautiful but limited range of blues.

Further reading

Christina Z. Anderson's books (e.g. Cyanotype: The Blueprint in Contemporary Practice), Peter Mrhar's Easy Digital Negatives site and manual, and Mark Nelson's Precision Digital Negatives cover calibration in depth.
